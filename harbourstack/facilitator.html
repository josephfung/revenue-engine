Maturity Assessment

Likely diagnosis

DimensionStageRationale
Market Fit 2 — Emerging Strong-fit customers exist, but ICP is still too loose. The pattern is visible but not named or operationalized.
Sales Process 3 — Structured CRM stages, pipeline reviews, dashboards, and sales leadership are in place. The process exists, even if it is not solving the right problem.
Enablement 1 — Ad Hoc New reps cannot carry the founder's story, qualify deeply, or move deals without founder involvement. Knowledge still lives inside the founder.
Technology 3 — Structured CRM, dashboards, call recording, and reporting are in place. Tooling is not the main bottleneck.
Retention & Expansion 2 — Emerging Some CS motion exists, but expectations are unclear, expansion is weak, and churn is rising. The lifecycle is not managed intentionally.
Trap answer

They need better sales execution.

Better answer

They scaled the sales motion before systematizing founder knowledge and tightening the ICP.

The secondary issue is Retention & Expansion. They are closing customers who should not have been sold in the first place, then handing those customers to CS with fuzzy expectations — creating churn, weak expansion, and poor feedback loops.

The bottleneck

Enablement + Market Fit, with Retention & Expansion showing the downstream damage.

The imbalance

HarbourStack has:

  • Enough process to create activity
  • Enough technology to measure the activity
  • Not enough ICP clarity to focus the team
  • Not enough enablement to make reps effective
  • Not enough retention discipline to make growth compound

The company has built sales infrastructure, but not yet a real revenue engine.

What Happened Next

Act 1: They misdiagnosed the problem

HarbourStack initially followed the board's instinct. They hired two more AEs, increased paid spend by 60%, and bought a better outbound platform.

For a quarter, the numbers looked better — more meetings, more demos, more pipeline, more CRM activity. But the underlying problem got worse.

After two quarters:

  • CAC increased by 42%
  • Net new ARR barely moved
  • Sales cycles stayed above 90 days
  • Discounting increased again
  • Two AEs missed quota and left
  • Customer Success became overloaded
  • Gross revenue churn rose to 19%
  • NRR dropped below 90%
Painful lesson

They added fuel to a leaky engine.

Act 2: They pivoted to the real fix

HarbourStack paused hiring and cut paid spend back to its previous level. Then they spent one quarter on three fixes.

1. They narrowed the ICP

After analyzing wins, churn, implementation time, expansion, support load, and sales cycle length, they discovered their best customers were not simply "mid-sized logistics companies."

Real ICP

Regional field service companies with 75 to 300 employees, multiple branches, high compliance burden, and a dispatch team still operating partly in spreadsheets.

2. They turned founder knowledge into enablement

They built a practical sales curriculum:

  • Discovery questions by persona
  • Good-fit / bad-fit qualification guide
  • Demo paths by use case
  • Objection handling
  • Pricing and discounting guardrails
  • Implementation expectation checklist
  • Three annotated founder-led sales calls

The founder still helped on strategic deals, but was no longer the only person who could make the story land.

3. They repaired the Sales-to-CS handoff

Sales and CS created a shared definition of a clean sale. No deal could close without:

  • Confirmed business outcome
  • Named executive sponsor
  • Implementation owner
  • First success milestone
  • Known risks
  • Expansion hypothesis

Reveal

HarbourStack did not have a sales capacity problem. It had a revenue engine imbalance.

They had enough process to create activity, enough technology to measure it, but not enough ICP clarity or enablement to make the activity productive.
